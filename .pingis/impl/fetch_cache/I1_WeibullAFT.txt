<p id="mwAw">In the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics" title="Statistics" id="mwBA">statistical</a> area of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_analysis" title="Survival analysis" id="mwBQ">survival analysis</a>, an <b id="mwBg">accelerated failure time model </b> (<b id="mwBw">AFT model</b>) is a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_statistics" title="Parametric statistics" id="mwCA">parametric</a> model that provides an alternative to the commonly used <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_hazards_models" title="Proportional hazards models" class="mw-redirect" id="mwCQ">proportional hazards models</a>. Whereas a proportional hazards model assumes that the effect of a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariate" title="Covariate" class="mw-redirect" id="mwCg">covariate</a> is to multiply the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_rate" title="Hazard rate" class="mw-redirect" id="mwCw">hazard</a> by some constant, an AFT model assumes that the effect of a covariate is to accelerate or decelerate the life course of a disease by some constant. There is strong basic science evidence from <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans" title="Caenorhabditis elegans" id="mwDA"><i id="mwDQ">C. elegans</i></a> experiments by Stroustrup et al.<sup about="#mwt4" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-1"}}'><a href="#cite_note-1" id="mwDg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwDw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwEA">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwEQ">]</span></span></a></sup> indicating that AFT models are the correct model for biological survival processes.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="2" id="mwRQ" aria-labelledby="Statistical_issues"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Statistical_issues">Statistical issues</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Accelerated_failure_time_model&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Statistical issues"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwRg">Unlike proportional hazards models, in which <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cox_(statistician)" title="David Cox (statistician)" id="mwRw">Cox</a>'s semi-parametric proportional hazards model is more widely used than parametric models, AFT models are predominantly fully parametric  i.e. a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distribution" title="Probability distribution" id="mwSA">probability distribution</a> is specified for <span class="mwe-math-element mwe-math-element-inline" typeof="mw:Extension/math" about="#mwt32" id="mwSQ" data-mw='{"name":"math","attrs":{},"body":{"extsrc":"\\log(T_0)"}}'><span class="mwe-math-mathml-a11y" style="display: none;"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" class="mathjax_ignore" alttext="{\displaystyle \log(T_{0})}">

(Buckley and James<sup about="#mwt35" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-3"}}'><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwSg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwSw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTA">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTQ">]</span></span></a></sup> proposed a semi-parametric AFT but its use is relatively uncommon in applied research; in a 1992 paper, Wei<sup about="#mwt38" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-4" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-4"}}'><a href="#cite_note-4" id="mwTg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwTw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUA">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUQ">]</span></span></a></sup> pointed out that the Buckley–James model has no theoretical justification and lacks robustness, and reviewed alternatives.) This can be a problem, if a degree of realistic detail is required for modelling the distribution of a baseline lifetime. Hence, technical developments in this direction would be highly desirable.</p>

<p id="mwUg">When a frailty term is incorporated in the survival model, the regression parameter estimates from AFT models are robust to omitted <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariate" title="Covariate" class="mw-redirect" id="mwUw">covariates</a>, unlike proportional hazards models. They are also less affected by the choice of probability distribution for the frailty term.<sup about="#mwt41" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-5" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-5"}}'><a href="#cite_note-5" id="mwVA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwVQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVg">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVw">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt44" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-6" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-6"}}'><a href="#cite_note-6" id="mwWA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwWQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWg">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwXA">The results of AFT models are easily interpreted.<sup about="#mwt47" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-7" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-7"}}'><a href="#cite_note-7" id="mwXQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwXg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXw">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwYA">]</span></span></a></sup> For example, the results of a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial" title="Clinical trial" id="mwYQ">clinical trial</a> with mortality as the endpoint could be interpreted as a certain percentage increase in future <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy" title="Life expectancy" id="mwYg">life expectancy</a> on the new treatment compared to the control. So a patient could be informed that he would be expected to live (say) 15% longer if he took the new treatment. <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio" title="Hazard ratio" id="mwYw">Hazard ratios</a> can prove harder to explain in layman's terms.</p>

<section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwZA" aria-labelledby="Distributions_used_in_AFT_models"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Distributions_used_in_AFT_models">Distributions used in AFT models</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Accelerated_failure_time_model&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Distributions used in AFT models"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwZQ">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-logistic_distribution" title="Log-logistic distribution" id="mwZg">log-logistic distribution</a> provides the most commonly used AFT model<link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:All_articles_with_unsourced_statements" about="#mwt48" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwZw" data-mw='{"parts":[{"template":{"target":{"wt":"Citation needed","href":"./Template:Citation_needed"},"params":{"reason":{"wt":"Weibull is the default in the R survival package"},"date":{"wt":"May 2023"}},"i":0}}]}'/><link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:Articles_with_unsourced_statements_from_May_2023" about="#mwt48"/><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;" about="#mwt48" id="mwaA"><span typeof="mw:Entity">[</span><i><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="Weibull is the default in the R survival package (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i><span typeof="mw:Entity">]</span></sup>. Unlike the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibull_distribution" title="Weibull distribution" id="mwaQ">Weibull distribution</a>, it can exhibit a non-<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonic" title="Monotonic" class="mw-redirect" id="mwag">monotonic</a> hazard function which increases at early times and decreases at later times. It is somewhat similar in shape to the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-normal_distribution" title="Log-normal distribution" id="mwaw">log-normal distribution</a> but it has heavier tails. The log-logistic <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_distribution_function" title="Cumulative distribution function" id="mwbA">cumulative distribution function</a> has a simple <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-form_expression" title="Closed-form expression" id="mwbQ">closed form</a>, which becomes important computationally when fitting data with <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censoring_(statistics)" title="Censoring (statistics)" id="mwbg">censoring</a>. <p id="mwcA">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibull_distribution" title="Weibull distribution" id="mwcQ">Weibull distribution</a> (including the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_distribution" title="Exponential distribution" id="mwcg">exponential distribution</a> as a special case) can be parameterised as either a proportional hazards model or an AFT model, and is the only family of distributions to have this property. The results of fitting a Weibull model can therefore be interpreted in either framework. However, the biological applicability of this model may be limited by the fact that the hazard function is monotonic, i.e. either decreasing or increasing.</p>

<p id="mwcw">Any distribution on a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicatively_closed_set" title="Multiplicatively closed set" id="mwdA">multiplicatively closed group</a>, such as the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_real_numbers" title="Positive real numbers" id="mwdQ">positive real numbers</a>, is suitable for an AFT model. Other distributions include the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-normal_distribution" title="Log-normal distribution" id="mwdg">log-normal</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_distribution" title="Gamma distribution" id="mwdw">gamma</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertabastic_survival_models" title="Hypertabastic survival models" id="mweA">hypertabastic</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gompertz_distribution" title="Gompertz distribution" id="mweQ">Gompertz distribution</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_Gaussian_distribution" title="Inverse Gaussian distribution" id="mweg">inverse Gaussian distributions</a>, although they are less popular than the log-logistic, partly as their cumulative distribution functions do not have a closed form. Finally, the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_gamma_distribution" title="Generalized gamma distribution" id="mwew">generalized gamma distribution</a> is a three-parameter distribution that includes the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibull_distribution" title="Weibull distribution" id="mwfA">Weibull</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-normal_distribution" title="Log-normal distribution" id="mwfQ">log-normal</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_distribution" title="Gamma distribution" id="mwfg">gamma</a> distributions as special cases.</p>
